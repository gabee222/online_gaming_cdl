Online Gaming

Online gaming is defined as playing an electronic game with some visual interface and input device to generate visual feedback on a screen, interacting with people on the internet. On this page, we will analyze how people participate in online gaming in daily life, how someone personally gets involved in online gaming (and how I personally interacted with it), and how we can consider each part of the term Critical Digital Literacy (CDL).

Throughout this course, we have analyzed quite a few news articles, videos, and podcasts that talk about video gaming. Specifically, I am going to go through Co-playing: video games (NYTimes article), Gaming has benefits and perils - parents can help kids by playing with them, and a few related articles that bring concepts that are useful for interpreting our digestion of video games. These artifacts talk about how these forms of media, sometimes naming video gaming specifically, help us understand, critically think, and participate in technology as a whole.

Introduction

What do we know about video gaming?

Online gaming has become quite a phenomenon in the past decade. While it was a niche hobby in the past, it has become a widespread pastime that everyone, especially the younger generation, engages in while resting at home. Therefore, even more so in the modern era, it is important to think critically about how we are engaging with this media. Understanding the benefits and disadvantages of online gaming is important to the way our society develops and evolves.

Daily Life

How do people engage with online gaming in their daily lives?

Online gaming is usually an activity that people do after class or work. One of the benefits of online gaming is that you can hang out and have fun with your friends from anywhere in the world, meaning that when you go home and just want to relax, it is easy to just play some online games with your friends. As demonstrated in the video Why AI will never replace a great teacher, the key aspect of it is the fact that AI can never really replace real human connection. It can make things more accessible and create shortcuts for people to learn, but face-to-face human interaction is what it cannot replace. One of the ways that people receive this connection, even while being halfway across the world, could be through gaming. It is an easy way to have fun with people without requiring you to be physically with them. I think this is at the core of online gaming: the ease of connection it brings, allowing strong online communities. People can also game online together in person. In one case, as seen in Co-playing: video games and Gaming has benefits and perils - parents can help kids by playing with them, online gaming is used as a fun way to teach kids about important concepts like problem-solving and collaboration.

Involvement

How do people personally get involved in online gaming?

For a lot of people, gaming became popularized because of hit games that people play with their friends (e.g. Roblox, Fortnite, Minecraft). That is also how I got introduced to gaming as well, because my friends really liked playing Minecraft. However, because my gaming hobby was never really encouraged by my parents, I never really got any guidance on how to navigate the internet. Because of that, I feel like I've had to work hard to navigate the internet without getting my attention sucked away.

We can see that a lot of parents encourage kids to explore their passions, and one of them can be online gaming. We see this in Connected Learning with Mimi Ito, where she teaches us about how instead of being restrictive on her kid's gaming interest, she actively tries to engage with him in it and understand why he is so interested. She also explains that her program is meant to encourage kids to explore their passions while also connecting with other people and looking for opportunities. She calls this connected learning, where interest, community, and opportunity all intersect. This is another way that specifically kids can get into online gaming and engage with it in a healthy manner. The example Mimi Ito gave in the talk was of a kid who really liked playing Minecraft and found a lot of friends that way.

Critical Digital Literacy

What makes this a Critical Digital Literacy (CDL)?

Why is it critical?

Online gaming is critical because it involves who has the power to shape your experience while gaming. Large corporations are specifically designing their games to keep maximum retention in their games. That is also why it is important to teach kids how to critically think about technology, as discussed in the TED Talk How to raise kids who question AI. In the TED Talk, she talks about how she taught a kid to critically think about how to question technology. In the same way, people also need to question how companies are using online games to exploit the consumer for monetization.

Why is it digital?

Online games are specifically more dangerous than traditional board games or physical games because they are purposefully intended to hack the dopamine receptors for maximum retention. Their digital presence allows them to really optimize for this via data personalization, live service and continuous updates, and psychological habit loops. An example of these algorithms in play would be loot boxes, regular content drops, push notifications, and daily rewards.

What makes it a literacy?

Online gaming is considered a literacy because it involves both consumers and producers. Online gaming has developed a massive learned culture surrounding the hobby, and learning these cues is important. That is why it is also beneficial to teach kids who like to game how to get into the gaming culture safely. Of course, it is impossible to go through everything about the culture of gaming, since each genre has its own niche; however, it's important to teach kids general rules to approach their gaming hobby.
